NORA · Nanopore Oncology Reasoning Agent

Reading cancer's molecular signal, in hours, not weeks.

Cancer diagnosis still takes weeks, and while patients wait, aggressive tumors advance. The genomic references clinicians rely on were built mostly from non-Saudi populations, so they miss variants that matter here. This project addresses both problems at once: nanopore long-read sequencing reads the molecular signal of cancer directly from a single sample, and one explainable AI model turns that signal into three answers together, the diagnosis, the molecular subtype, and the treatment direction, in 6 to 18 hours instead of weeks.

That model lives inside NORA, a larger Saudi-led platform for multi-cancer early detection built on nanopore data and reasoning AI agents. Its first working application, NORA-EAC, detects esophageal cancer early from a routine blood draw, where the agents reason over cell-free RNA and explain every conclusion a clinician sees. The approach is grounded in a decade of published cancer and deep-learning research, and follows a nanopore program already deployed across Norway's hospitals, adapting that proven path into a Saudi-owned precision-oncology capability under Vision 2030.

The unmet need

Saudi cancer intelligence cannot be inferred from global data alone.

The barrier is not only sequencing. It is the absence of a locally validated pathway from sample to clinical decision. While patients wait for molecular results, aggressive cancers progress and treatment options narrow.

Data

Under-represented genomes

International datasets may not capture Saudi genomic and epigenomic characteristics, producing high rates of variants of uncertain significance and reducing confidence in local use.

Workflow

Fragmented pipeline

Sequencing, clinical annotation, analysis and validation are often disconnected. Sample batching creates bottlenecks for exactly the urgent cases that can least afford the delay.

Evidence

Clinical evidence gap

A local nanopore and AI model needs Saudi samples, governance, and prospective clinical validation before it can responsibly inform care.

Why nanopore and AI

One sample. Multiple layers of clinical evidence.

Long-read nanopore sequencing reads native molecules directly, capturing signal that short-read, amplification-based workflows discard. AI turns that richer signal into decisions a clinician can interrogate.

Long-read molecular signals

Capture complex structural and sequence variation in a single rapid, real-time sequencing run, with no batching required.

Genomic and epigenomic features

Native reads expose methylation and modifications alongside sequence, giving richer representations from the very same sample.

Explainable AI

Predictions are tied to interpretable molecular evidence, so clinicians can see why, not just what.

Multilabel clinical output

One model supports diagnosis, subtype, stratification and treatment-related insight together, instead of a chain of separate assays.

The nanopore and AI multilabel system

From one tumor or liquid-biopsy sample to three coordinated outputs.

The platform, NORA

NORA: an agentic AI platform for multi-cancer early detection.

NORA is a Saudi-led platform where specialized AI agents read long-read nanopore data and reason toward early cancer detection. It is built to grow cancer by cancer: every new application reuses the same agent framework, sequencing pipeline and explainable-AI core, so the platform compounds with each cohort it learns from.

Methodology and data

Built on public long-read cfRNA, analyzed honestly

Development explores GSE271389, an Oxford Nanopore PromethION cell-free RNA plasma cohort spanning three groups. The primary fragmentomics question: does the cfRNA fragment-length distribution separate groups under honest nested cross-validation?

Rigor by design: no quality filtering in the primary analysis, because filtering biases the measurement, and flow-cell effects (plate row) are modeled as a covariate, since read length is instrument-driven, not only disease-driven.

A Saudi pathway, modeled on a proven rollout

From feasibility to national precision oncology.

A staged 24-month research plan, structured on a pathway already proven at national scale. In Norway, Oxford Nanopore and MATRIX validated nanopore tumour classification on an initial cohort, deployed it across three hospitals, and are pursuing IVDR approval by the end of 2026: a working blueprint for a national program (Oxford Nanopore and MATRIX, Norway).

Phase 00 to 6 months

Set the foundation

Clinical partner, cohort definition, ethics approval, data governance, and a standardized nanopore sequencing SOP.

Phase 16 to 12 months

Build the pilot and evaluate

Generate the first Saudi cohort, train baseline multilabel models, build the prototype, and run technical verification against reference diagnostics.

Phase 212 to 24 months

Validate and expand across sites

Clinical comparison, model refinement, and a second cohort or site to test generalization beyond a single center.

Phase 324 months and beyond

Regulatory and national integration

A regulatory pathway and integration with national healthcare systems, building sovereign genomic capability and Saudi-owned IP under Vision 2030 and the National Biotechnology Strategy.
